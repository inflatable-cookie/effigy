A plain foreground admitted task now has a finite, source-enforced persistence footprint. Its gate files are a fixed set (the task's gate and its checkout-alias spelling, plus the v2 store writer gate) written only through one bounded writer, so a `.gate` body is at most 4096 bytes and a `.scope` marker stays empty; a foreground admission refuses more than four aliased gates. Under admission protocol v2 the one terminal record row validates its identities instead of cutting them — an oversized `run_id`, `checkout`, `boot`, `outcome` or `cleanup` is refused and the record is not written — cuts only the descriptive task label with a `[truncated]` marker, and refuses any encoded row over its 960-byte maximum, which keeps the row inside one page; its expired-row prune is a bounded slice. The store open applies and reads back `journal_size_limit` and `wal_autocheckpoint` and refuses an open whose settings did not apply, and a closing writable store truncates its write-ahead log inside a short window. The per-invocation log claim is now the contribution one invocation makes — the frames of its own bounded transaction, measured with a reader pinning the log — rather than a peak the shared log cannot be held to; contract 049 states the contribution, its enforced terms and what it excludes (effigy#977).
